## Wording to use

1. **Ridge before costs.** Not "never significant". Its gross alpha is not
   significant against FF5 and momentum on the first-day execution (t = 1.22
   and 1.54), but its t-statistic is above 2 against CAPM, FF3 and FF5 in most
   cells. After costs nothing changes: no positive and significant net alpha.
2. **"Gross" / "before costs".** It is the net return plus the costs paid, on
   the positions really held. Do not write "even at zero cost" or "a fund
   without costs". The value 28.6 is a gross index, not a fund.
3. **Exploration (patience rule).** Always say it was done after the result of
   version A was seen: 52 configurations explored on the same years; its
   t-statistics are descriptive. "Not significantly different from the market"
   does not mean "equal to the market".
4. **Why the patience rule helps** (new measure, same 396 months, trees,
   patience of 12 months against the frozen rule): net return +3.97 points a
   year, of which +4.04 are costs no longer paid and −0.07 is gross return;
   the volatility of the gap to the market falls from 5.9 % to 2.0 % a year.
   The gain is the costs avoided; better selection is not established.
5. **Ridge and slower trading.** "Its gross alpha disappears" holds for the
   patience rule only (2.09 % to about zero). With orders once a year it stays
   at 2.36 % (t = 1.60), with much cash in the first years.
6. **Not to claim.** That the forecasts hold no information, or that
   predictability declined after 2009: neither is measured. A flat validation
   loss does not compare the model with a constant forecast.
7. **Costs and the market.** Over 1993–2025 the costs are 98 % of the gap to
   the market; after 2009, 34 %. These shares concern the mean return against
   the market, not the factor-adjusted alpha.
